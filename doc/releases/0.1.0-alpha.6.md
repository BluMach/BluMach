# BluMach 0.1.0-alpha.6 — classic Windows preview

Sixth experimental preview of the classic Qt 6 product. Downloads are listed
on the
[GitHub release page](https://github.com/BluMach/BluMach/releases/tag/v0.1.0-alpha.6).
This preview is the first to include the CoreMach engine, which runs the
Olivetti M10. Every other machine still runs on the classic emulator.

## Changes since alpha.5

- **Olivetti M10 on CoreMach.** The 1983 8-bit portable (OKI 80C85, 8 to
  32 KB of battery-backed RAM, 240 × 64 LCD) can be created from the
  catalogue and runs on the CoreMach engine. These configurations never
  start through the classic core.
  - Creation offers 8, 16, 24 or 32 KB of RAM, the keyboard language jumpers
    (Italian, French, German, British) and the clock start: host time,
    running on while the machine is closed, or a fixed start. RAM and clock
    are kept in the machine's `state/` folder.
  - The machine window gives pause, reset, Switch on after the automatic
    power-off, sound, the serial port, the cassette and the printer in
    Action, and the scale and an optional LCD look in View.
  - Sound for BEEP and SOUND. The serial port (`COM:`) sends a text file at
    the programmed line rate and saves what it receives. CSAVE and CLOAD use
    WAV tapes, and recordings are saved while the motor runs. LPRINT and
    LLIST save each printout as a text file in the machine's `printer`
    folder.
  - Settings is not available for CoreMach machines: create a new machine
    to change its RAM or jumpers.
- **M10 catalogue sheet** in the five languages, in the Olivetti M family,
  with its history, the Kyocera relationship and an engineering narrative.
- **Catalogue photographs.** The Olivetti M10, M15, M28, Prodest PC 1 and
  PC 1 HD, Amstrad PC1512 and Toshiba T3200 sheets show a freely licensed
  photograph with its author and licence; clicking the image opens its
  source page. Authors and licences are listed in the
  [catalogue image notes](../../src/qt/catalog/images/README.md).
- **Release packages are built in CI** from the version tag with the same
  script as before, always with CoreMach. Each ZIP has a SHA-256 file, a
  manifest that records the BluMach and CoreMach commits, and a build
  provenance attestation:
  `gh attestation verify BluMach-0.1.0-alpha.6-Windows-x86_64.zip --repo BluMach/BluMach`.

## Setup and firmware

The [alpha.1 setup guide](0.1.0-alpha.1.md) still describes ZIP extraction,
the independent BluMach VM library, configuration filenames and root ROM
folder selection. Each machine sheet identifies its required firmware and
creation choices. Place entitled local firmware under the root ROM folder
selected in Preferences.

The M10 needs the first 32 KB of its system ROM at
`roms/machines/olivetti_m10/m10-system-first32k.bin`, SHA-256
`43f85e3ac5cc264be3ad4c3b36dea82381409a66381146ce06a78c22704b0dbc`.

ROMs, BIOS, operating systems, applications and private preservation inputs
must not be included in source or binary release assets.

## Known limits

- M10: the bar-code reader, the US modem variant, the low-battery warning
  and the 256 bytes of RAM in the 81C55 are not emulated. The serial port
  works per character, without BREAK or RTS, DTR and CTS. Timing is computed
  per instruction, not per bus cycle. A tape recorded on a real M10 and the
  BEEP tone against the machine remain to be checked.
- The limits listed for alpha.5 still apply to the other machines.
- The new machines are experimental or partial unless their sheet states
  otherwise.

## Validation and follow-up

Automated catalogue and configuration tests are regression checks, not proof
of complete hardware fidelity. Checked during preparation with the original
M10 ROM (not distributed): menu and BASIC with the bytes free the manual
gives for 8, 16, 24 and 32 KB; RAM and clock across sessions; automatic
power-off after ten idle minutes; BEEP and SOUND; SAVE and LOAD through
`COM:` at 9600 bit/s; CSAVE and CLOAD on tape, including an 11025 Hz WAV;
LPRINT and LLIST.

Report bugs with version, host, model, BIOS selection, RAM, storage geometry
and reproduction steps. Enable a startup log with `--logfile path`
(`-L path`) when useful.
